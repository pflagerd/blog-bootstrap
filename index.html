The manual is also kind of useful. I used it when I assembled the machine in the first place.

Contrary to what seems completely obvious, one does not put the distance gauge tool directly under the laser, but on the corner of the laser:

You can see from the manual that this is correct, but intuition says you should put the tool directly under the laser:

Also contrary to intuition, and other CNC device conventions, LaserGRBL seems to always start from the upper-right of the workpiece (a bit of partly-used scrap plywood in this case).

It seems to be good practise to use the "Go to top right" button before attempting any engraving.

Note that this caused the laser etcher to make a kind of "grindy" sound like it had reached a limit and was stuck.

I'm guessing there is a better way.

Thu Sep 3 10:18:14 AM PDT 2026 by Daniel

I remeasured the pieces of wood I'll be using. There are three identical pieces.

Here is the height. It's about 184.3mm. I'll call it 184 when I put it into the .svg file. Notice that the right side of the wood piece is not visible. That's because the camera is nearly perfectly centered above the right edge and so the measurement is more accurate than had the camera been too far left or right. I started with the camera positioned too far right such that I could clearly see the right side of the piece of wood, then moved the camera left until the edge disappeared. That way I knew the camera was nearly directly above the edge.

Here is the width. It's about 256mm.

Artifact Description
Candid phone photo of BC (timestamped 2019-01-11) dozing on someone's lap next to a computer desk — one of many casual reference photos, not staged for anything.
BC napping face-first against a couch cushion. Another "sweet sleepy face" candidate shot.
BC sprawled on her back on a blanket, completely conked out. A favorite goofy sleeping pose.
BC curled up asleep on a paisley bedspread next to a flea comb — looks like she'd just been groomed.
BC asleep on someone's lap at a computer desk (keyboard/mouse visible) — a "keeping me company while I work" shot.
BC curled up on the bed at night, eyes catching the camera flash.
Close, low-light portrait of BC looking straight into the camera — the kind of shot that'd crop well into a small oval, like the cameo in drawing.svg below.
BC sitting alert on the hallway rug, ears up, watching whoever's holding the camera.
A simple red heart clip-art icon (the filename suggests it was pulled from a "corazón"/heart icon set) — a reusable design element for the memorial artwork.
An early, unfinished layout draft for the plaque artwork: a grey placeholder oval (for a photo), the heart icon, and a grey placeholder rectangle (for the nameplate text) — before any real photo or text was dropped in.
A more finished version of the mock-up above: a photo of BC cropped into the oval, an outlined heart, and a nameplate reading "B.C. / 2007 - 2023" — looks like the working design file for a photo memorial piece.
A small glass vial with metal end-caps, photographed outdoors on a wire grate. Guessing this is a keepsake capsule (fur, ashes, or a note) meant to be kept with the memorial.
The actual engraved metal plaque: "In loving memory of BC, 2007 - 2023" with small cat icons. This looks like the finished/ordered memorial plaque itself — simpler than the photo-based drawing.svg concept, so maybe that design got dropped in favor of this one.
Patrick's first pass at turning a candid photo of BC into an engraving-ready image: an oil-paint/posterize filter flattens it into black/grey/white regions for CNC or laser etching. Her black fur and the dark background still blend together here, which is the "catblob" problem he flagged.
A cleaner decal/sticker design: BC drawn as a black-and-white line illustration, curled up asleep inside a heart outline — a different, more graphic-design-y direction than the photo-based etching attempt above.
