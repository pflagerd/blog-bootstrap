I had to make a couple of corrections to the script, but it worked as coded.

However, it exposed a problem. Since I'm cutting from bottom to top, given the size of the bit, there's a plunge into the material which occurs at the bottom (x == 0) of each vertical cut.

This plunge, although at only 50mm/min still causes the wood to "burn" as you can see from the video below.

Notice that before the gantry covers the bottom edge, the wood has no brown "burn", and after the gantry clears that column, it has a brown "burn" mark.

Also notice that the pitch of the blade drops sharply during the part of the plunge where the blade makes contact with the wood:

So i wrote a new script cam_scripts/top-level-top-to-bottom-left-to-right.py

This script should generate gcode which will cause the cutting head not to plunge into the wood at all.

Instead the cutting head will plunge to depth well above the top edge of the workpiece before it hits any wood.

Then it will cut from top to bottom, lifting after it reaches the bottom edge.

I'll test this out tomorrow hopefully.

2. Generate G-code. This is the CAM step, and it’s where you specify tool diameter, cut depth, feeds/speeds, stepdown per pass, and whether the toolpath is inside/outside/on the line (offset direction matters — for cutting a shape out, you want an outside offset by the tool radius, plus tabs so the piece doesn’t move mid-cut). Good Linux-native options:
  • a. FreeCAD Path workbench — heaviest but most capable. Import the SVG, extrude to a body if needed, then create a Job, Profile operation, and post-process with the grbl post. Best if you’ll be doing this often.
  • b. Kiri:Moto (grid.space/kiri) — runs in the browser, no install, has a CNC mode with good SVG import. Fast to learn.
  • c. Inkscape gcodetools extension (Extensions → Gcodetools) — stays in Inkscape but the UX is dated and it’s easy to get wrong offsets. Fine for engraving; less pleasant for profile cuts.
  • d. Estlcam runs well under Wine if you want something closer to what the Genmitsu community typically uses.
  • e. jscut was suggested by claude.ai in one of our conversations.
For a first cut in wood with a common 1/8" (3.175 mm) 2-flute endmill, reasonable starting numbers: feed 800–1000 mm/min, plunge 200–300 mm/min, stepdown 1–1.5 mm per pass, spindle at whatever fixed speed your 4030 spindle runs. Add 3–4 tabs (0.5–1 mm thick) around the perimeter.

Another important thing I learned is how to Home the LC-50's gantry.

Turns out that when you power up the LC-50, it'll often refuse to Home (or Test) until you send it a $X (unlock) command.

One reason it may refuse is that some error condition, or an E-Stop, occurred. It will retain the error state and refuse to Home (or Test) until sent a $X (unlock) command.

So I used Universal GCode Sender (ugsplatform) to connect to the LC-50 and then sent it the $X (unlock) command.

Then if I pressed Test/Home, a blue light would illuminate on the laser PCB and the laser itself would turn on (low power).

Pressing Test/Home again would cause the laser to turn off, but the blue light remained on.

Pressing and holding Home for about 5 seconds would cause the gantry to return to the Home position: the lower left corner of the engraving area.

Then I could terminate ugsplatform and connect using LaserGRBL.

I used bitblt with the default settings with power set to 10%.
